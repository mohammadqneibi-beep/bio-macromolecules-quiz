Guided discovery questions

1. What would happen to a cell if its lysosomes stopped working?

Worn-out organelles and engulfed material could not be broken down, so undigested molecules would accumulate inside the cell. This is what happens in lysosomal storage diseases such as Tay-Sachs disease (lipid build-up in neurons) and I-cell disease (enzymes are not tagged by the Golgi and never reach lysosomes).

2. Why do muscle cells contain many more mitochondria than most other cells?

Muscle contraction needs large and continuous amounts of ATP. Mitochondria produce most of the cell’s ATP through aerobic respiration, so cells with high energy demand (skeletal and heart muscle) contain many mitochondria.

3. A pancreatic cell secretes large amounts of the protein insulin. Which organelles would you expect to be especially prominent, and in what order does insulin pass through them?

Rough ER (synthesis on bound ribosomes and folding) → transport vesicles → Golgi apparatus (modification and packaging) → secretory vesicles → plasma membrane (exocytosis). Rough ER and Golgi are therefore abundant.

4. Liver cells of a person who regularly drinks alcohol show a large increase in one organelle. Which one, and why?

Smooth ER — it contains detoxifying enzymes (e.g., cytochrome P450). Repeated exposure to alcohol and drugs causes proliferation of smooth ER, which also increases tolerance to some medicines.
